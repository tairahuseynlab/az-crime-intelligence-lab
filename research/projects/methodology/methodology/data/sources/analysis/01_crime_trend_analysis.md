# Crime Trend Analysis: Azerbaijan, 1993–2024

## Research Question

How did the number and population-adjusted rate of recorded crimes in Azerbaijan change between 1993 and 2024?

## Data Source

The dataset is based on the State Statistical Committee of the Republic of Azerbaijan publication "Offences in Azerbaijan".

The analysis uses two indicators:

- Total number of recorded crimes
- Recorded crimes per 100,000 population

## Key Observations

The number of recorded crimes changed substantially over the period examined.

Recorded crime reached a low point in the early 2000s, followed by a general increase during the following years. The highest recorded total in this dataset occurred in 2023, with 36,811 recorded crimes.

In 2024, the number decreased to 33,379.

The population-adjusted rate shows a similar long-term pattern. It reached 173 recorded crimes per 100,000 population in 2000 and increased to 363 per 100,000 in 2023. In 2024, the rate decreased to 327 per 100,000.

## Analytical Interpretation

The data indicate substantial changes in recorded crime levels over the study period.

However, changes in recorded crime should not automatically be interpreted as changes in the underlying level of criminal behavior. Recorded crime can also be affected by reporting practices, law-enforcement activity, legislation, population changes, and other factors.

Therefore, this analysis identifies statistical patterns rather than establishing causal explanations.

## 2021–2024 Observation

A notable increase is visible between 2020 and 2022:

- 2020: 26,004 recorded crimes
- 2021: 31,131
- 2022: 36,494
- 2023: 36,811
- 2024: 33,379

This period should be examined separately in future research to determine which crime categories contributed most to the change.

## Limitations

This analysis is based on recorded crime data. It does not measure unreported or undetected crime.

The dataset also does not, by itself, establish why crime levels changed.

Further analysis should examine individual crime categories, regional distribution, demographic characteristics, and longer-term patterns.
