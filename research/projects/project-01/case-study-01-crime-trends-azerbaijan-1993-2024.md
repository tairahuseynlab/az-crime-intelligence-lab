# Case Study 01 — Recorded Crime Trends in Azerbaijan, 1993–2024

## Overview

This case study examines long-term patterns in recorded crime in Azerbaijan between 1993 and 2024.

The purpose is to demonstrate how official crime statistics can be organized, examined, and interpreted using basic criminal intelligence and crime-analysis principles.

## Research Question

How did recorded crime levels and population-adjusted crime rates in Azerbaijan change between 1993 and 2024?

## Data

The analysis uses two primary indicators:

- Total number of recorded crimes
- Recorded crimes per 100,000 population

The underlying data are based on the State Statistical Committee of the Republic of Azerbaijan publication *Offences in Azerbaijan*.

## Key Observations

Recorded crime was relatively low during the late 1990s and reached 13,958 cases in 2000.

The number of recorded crimes subsequently increased over the following years and reached 36,811 cases in 2023.

In 2024, the recorded total decreased to 33,379 cases.

The population-adjusted rate followed a broadly similar pattern, increasing from 173 crimes per 100,000 population in 2000 to 363 in 2023, before declining to 327 in 2024.

## Analytical Interpretation

These figures demonstrate a substantial change in recorded crime levels over the period examined.

However, recorded crime statistics should not automatically be interpreted as a direct measure of actual criminal activity.

Changes may also reflect differences in reporting, law-enforcement activity, legislation, population structure, and other institutional or social factors.

Therefore, this case study identifies patterns in recorded crime rather than claiming causal explanations.

## 2020–2024 Focus

The period from 2020 to 2024 shows a particularly notable change:

| Year | Recorded crimes |
|---|---:|
| 2020 | 26,004 |
| 2021 | 31,131 |
| 2022 | 36,494 |
| 2023 | 36,811 |
| 2024 | 33,379 |

The increase between 2020 and 2023 followed by a decrease in 2024 represents an area for further investigation.

## Intelligence Questions for Further Research

Future analysis should examine:

1. Which crime categories contributed most to the changes?
2. Did the pattern differ between regions?
3. Were demographic characteristics associated with particular trends?
4. Which years represent statistically unusual changes?
5. What institutional or social factors could plausibly explain observed patterns?

These questions require additional datasets before conclusions can be drawn.

## Limitations

The dataset represents recorded crime and does not capture crimes that were not reported or detected.

The available aggregate statistics also cannot independently establish causal relationships.
